# Oyatsu 

A widget to help you understand Oyatsu's time.

What is the time for Oyatsu? What is the afternoon? What is the ox 3 hours?
This is what it called because it was eaten at "Yatutoki" (that is, around 3pm in modern times). In modern times, it is sometimes called "Oyatsu at 3 o'clock."  
During the Edo period, dawn was Ake-Muttsu, or Yattsu: Oyatsu time was Futatsu from Ake-Muttsu is Yattsu. Hiru-Yattsu is Hitsuji-no-Koku. That is/was the time of Oyatsu. The afternoon in Uma-no-Koku to Ne-no-Koku.
Dawn is a U-Hitotsu.

## Function  

### Widget  
* Japanese calendar  
* Japanese-style month names  
* Gregorian date  
* Japanese clock time (seasonal time)  
* 24 solar terms  
* Chinese zodiac signs    

###  GUI  
* Japanese clock (seasonal time clock)  
* Latitude, Longitude setting  

Japanese clock time is calculated and displayed from position information: longitude and latitude.
If you do not set the location information (with the installation still), use Tokyo Station (`35.6895, 139.6917`).

## setting  

### Enter your location information  

#### Copy and paste latitude and longitude numbers from Google Maps etc.    

#### OsmAnd (Using the Open Street Maps application)  

OsmAnd is "**Global Mobile Map Viewing and Navigation for Offline and Online OSM Maps**" that can be used even in places without the Internet. You can choose a map that suits a variety of purposes, such as hiking, skiing, voyage, etc.  

[OsmAnd - Google Play Store](https://play.Download and install from [OsmAnd - Google Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=net.osmand&pcampaignid=web_share) or [OsmAnd - F-droid](https://f-droid.org/ja/packages/net.osmand.plus/) and then set geo:latitude and longitude to Oyatsu from the share.  

Select the location you want to know the time on the map. The time of a Japanese clock is calculated from the sunrise and sunrise at that location.  
<img alt="OsmAnd screenshot" src="metadata/en-US/images/phoneScreenshots/9.png" width="50%">  
Click Share to see which method to share.  
<img alt="OsmAnd screenshot" src="metadata/en-US/images/phoneScreenshots/10.png" width="50%">  
Select geo: to display which app you want to share with.   
<img alt="OsmAnd screenshot" src="metadata/en-US/images/phoneScreenshots/11.png" width="50%">  
If you select "Only once", the latitude and longitude of the selected point will be entered on the Oyatsu location information input screen.  
<img alt="OsmAnd screenshot" src="metadata/en-US/images/phoneScreenshots/12.png" width="50%">  
Press Apply.  

#### Using AAT   

AAT is a sport such as "GPS-tracking application for sportive activities, with emphasis on cycling." It also allows you to collect information such as location, speed, and height differences in sports such as cycling, hiking, and marathons. You can also choose from a variety of maps.   

[AAT - F-droid] Download and install from (https://f-droid.org/packages/ch.bailu.aat/) and then set geo:latitude and longitude to Oyatsu from the share.  
[AAT - Github](https://github.com/bailuk/AAT/tree/master) Here's the info.   

Launch it, go to MAP, and set the location you want to know the time on the map to the center of the screen (tapping at the bottom of the screen and pressing the square 1 to get to your current location), and then tapping the left side of the screen** will appear, and the menu will appear.   
<img alt="AAT screenshot" src="metadata/en-US/images/phoneScreenshots/5.png" width="50%">  
Select View location... in this marker item.  
<img alt="AAT screenshot" src="metadata/en-US/images/phoneScreenshots/6.png" width="50%">   
The latitude and longitude of the selected point is displayed in geo:latitude and longitude, and there is Oyatsu so select it.  
<img alt="AAT screenshot" src="metadata/en-US/images/phoneScreenshots/7.png" width="50%">  
Applying this will display the Japanese clock time at that point.  
<img alt="AAT screenshot" src="metadata/en-US/images/phoneScreenshots/8.png" width="50%">

### Dango

Source: Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries website [Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries Our Local Cuisine Kushidango Tokyo]](https://www.maff.go.jp/j/keikaku/syokubunka/k_ryouri/search_menu/menu/34_29_tokyo.html#:~:text=%E5%9B%A3%E5%AD%90%E3%81%AF%E5%B9%B3%E5%AE%89%E6%99%82%E4%BB%A3%E3%81%AB,%E5%A3%B2%E3%82%8B%E5%BA%97%E3%82%82%E3%81%A7%E3%81%8D%E3%81%9F%E3%80%82)

> The five-skewered dango became so wildly popular that it spawned the famous saying "Hana-yori Dango": Dango over cherry blossoms (because who needs pretty flowers when you've got delicious dumplings, right?), and this culinary sensation spread across the entire nation. Legend has it that this串-tastic treat originated in Kyoto.  

> Even in Tokyo during the Edo period, the five-skewer style reigned supreme, selling for a neat 5 mon coins per stick. But then came the plot twist! When 4 mon coins started circulating, chaos ensued. Sneaky customers began pulling the old "drop 4 coins and dash" routine, grabbing their dango and running off without paying the full price.  

> Shop owners were tearing their hair out over these dango bandits! In a stroke of desperate genius (or perhaps just plain exhaustion), they decided: "If you can't beat 'em, join 'em!" So they reduced the number of dango from five to four per skewer to match the 4 mon price. And thus, the four-skewered dango was born – not from culinary innovation, but from customers' creative accounting skills!

>To this day, this delicious divide persists: **Kanto region proudly sticks with 4 dango per skewer, while Kansai maintains the original 5-dango tradition**. It's like a tasty civil war that's been going on for centuries!
